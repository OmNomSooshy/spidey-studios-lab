## The judgment behind the campaign

The survey included IX source across the connected home, possessions, care, food, sleep, sensors, rope and expedition, plus an ordinary-RAF/trusted-touch run through shop treats, physical feeding, the walk home, actual supported sleep, dreams and a grab interruption. The useful gaps were relationships rather than another room or activity. Byte already had the tools to solve more of his own tiny problems. His player could provide care, but he did not ask for it. His sleep existed, but the house could not be quietly inhabited because any canvas touch woke him.

Four bounded answers survived implementation and play:

| Noticed → choice | Why it belongs / existing truth | What Human Fingers can experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dirt is a consequence, yet washing was entirely player-initiated → Byte notices himself and brings the actual sponge. | Existing dirt events, actual sponge, chest rummaging, physical retrieval rope, connected passages and expectant performance. No new need, penalty or compulsory care loop. | After meaningful dirty play or an expedition, an idle grubby Byte sniffs his own hand, finds his sponge (including inside the chest), fetches it, goes to the bathroom and offers it. Grab it and wash him normally, interrupt him, or ignore him. |
| Fetch and biscuit theft ignored each other → Byte can cheat at a high throw. | His existing scheming beat and the same moving-endpoint, pull-only room rope used for physical food theft. The toy remains a moving body; no decorative second web. | Answer his ball/ring invitation with a high throw. He can stop, plot, web the actual toy, reel it within physical reach, carry it back and invite another throw. |
| A toy was something to play with, not something to take home after playing → a recently played toy can accompany rest. | Actual recent toy memory, real carried possession, existing passages, actual nook and genuine self-authored sleep web. | When he chooses rest in the playroom and the recently played toy is loose and reachable, he picks it up, takes it to his nook, puts it down before casting his sleep web, then sleeps and dreams. The toy stays loose, grabbable and saved in that room. |
| Every object touch woke him → distinguish quiet household interaction from real noise. | Existing object ownership and pointer routing, physical prop impact speed, actual rattle motion, existing wake/nest release and authored sleep. | Quietly move a possession or use the wardrobe while he sleeps and dreams. Shake the actual rattle or cause a hard local impact: he wakes and gives an offended, heavy-lidded look. Touching/grabbing Byte and floor-follow intent still wake him normally. |

Two new HQ candidate poses — **sniff** and **huffy** — interpret these situations. The generated master is in `art/sunburn-x-acting-source.png`; deterministic preparation is `qa/prepare-x-art.py`. Both use the same 214×264 presentation reference and foot baseline. Art never defines collision bounds, movement, spool position, web reach or source state. They use existing clothing/contact overlays and silhouette-respecting dirt/wet coating. Human Eyes retains appearance authority.

## Physical and interruption authority

There is one existing room tether solver. New moving endpoints use its existing inverse-mass/tension machinery, progressive cast and reel; slack applies no force. Food theft remains the same physical sequence. The private historical API name `castFoodWeb` is retained rather than broadly renaming working source.

Care is a small local authored intention, not a general behavior framework. A **new real dirt event** opens an idle opportunity only when Byte is already sufficiently grubby. Initial dirt alone does not demand care. A request supplies neither water nor cleanliness. Its offered sponge is the same world object Human Fingers can grab. Ignoring it drops the sponge after the waiting beat and ends the act. New dirt can make a later request possible; cooldown avoids repeated nagging. There are no absence consequences.

Grabs, object handling, player webs, Earth mode, expedition entry and incompatible life activity cancel the relevant new intention. Cancellation drops a carried object through existing home rules and releases only the intention's moving-endpoint rope. Rope release and noisy wake do not zero linear/angular momentum. A grab dismisses the huffy beat immediately. Screen/Earth gravity acquisition, support, orientation and locomotion are unchanged.

Toy-to-bed only adapts an existing rest decision in the playroom, when a recent real toy is currently reachable and moving slowly. It does not create a sleep obligation, summon a stored toy from another room, or force all rest to use a companion. The loose toy can roll away naturally after being set down; this is a physical possession beside the nook, not a locked sleeping prop.

## Performance approach

X adds no full-screen layer, refraction work, particles or new world-rendering pass. The tiny intent state reuses existing pose/body caches and rope rendering. Crystal's bounded eight-tile / 9,960,040-byte protection remains unchanged. Matched IX/X software-rendered Chromium comparisons use DPR 2, ordinary and 6× CPU throttling, the same dressed/dirt-coated sleep and busy kitchen, and repeated scrolling Crystal passes. Treat samples as comparisons on this execution machine, not predictions of handset FPS. Extreme throttling still exposes the pre-existing expensive sleep scene.

Currency, ownership/equipment and returned fragments survive reload. No local browser error or failed runtime asset request remained.

## Rejected and deferred

- **A ceiling-hung ball toy:** rejected in favor of web-assisted fetch. A new prop suspension/reel setup would add competing anchor ownership and placement work; the existing food rope already provides a stronger, more characteristic joke with less machinery.
- **Automatic self-bathing:** rejected. Filling the tub and automatically making Byte clean would erase the care relationship. He can ask and bring the tool; Human Fingers still performs interrogable wet contact/scrubbing.
- **A fixed cuddling tableau:** rejected. Locking the toy onto a sleep sprite would replace possession truth. The real toy may roll or be stolen while he sleeps.
- **A pantry stash/secret hoard:** left unimplemented. It is attractive, but would add another food-carry/ownership policy after these four connected surprises already make the home richer.
- **Persistent preference scores / needs / general routine selection:** deliberately omitted. Existing recent-play memory and a bounded dirt-event opportunity are enough to suggest a mind; no new psychology or obligation system was earned.

Known limitations: quiet wardrobe/chest interaction is an intentional semantic correction; a loud dropped object can wake him even if it was not aimed at him. Noise is locally inferred from actual in-world motion, not acoustic simulation. Fetch is opportunistic and currently applies to ball/comet-ball/ring invitations, not every toy or every player throw. The care sniff uses candidate art; his offered sponge still uses the established approximate hand attachment. Bedside objects obey ordinary physics. Event frequency, whether asking for a bath suits his character, offended wake comedy and the new art all deserve Human Eyes / Human Fingers veto before canon.
